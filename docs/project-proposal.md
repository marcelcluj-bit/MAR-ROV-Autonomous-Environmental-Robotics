# MAR-ROV: Project Proposal & Technical Documentation
**Horizon Europe Framework – EIC Pathfinder Open / Cluster 4 & 6**

---

## 1. General Information
- **Project Title:** MAR-ROV: Autonomous Robotic System for Hazardous Waste Harvesting and Secure Submarine Storage in Hermetic Containers
- **Funding Pillar:** Horizon Europe – EIC Pathfinder Open / Cluster 4 & 6
- **Estimated Budget:** €5,500,000
- **Duration:** 36 Months

---

## 2. Executive Summary
The MAR-ROV project addresses the critical issue of hazardous waste (chemical, industrial, or low-level nuclear) threatening terrestrial ecosystems. The solution consists of a hybrid ecosystem: a fleet of autonomous underwater/terrestrial rovers (ROV/AUV) specialized in harvesting waste and sealing it inside smart, non-corrosive containers designed for high hydrostatic pressure. These containers are deployed in stable abyssal geological zones.

---

## 3. Problem & Objective Trees

### Problem Tree (Cause-Effect)
- **SUPREME EFFECT:** Irreversible contamination of terrestrial aquifers and human exposure to toxins/radiation.
  - *Direct Effect 1:* Current land storage is vulnerable to floods, earthquakes, and leaks.
  - *Direct Effect 2:* High risk of fatal accidents among human operators handling hazardous waste.
- **CENTRAL PROBLEM:** Inefficiency and hazardous nature of current long-term hazardous waste collection and storage systems.
  - *Direct Cause 1:* Lack of autonomous robotic systems capable of operating in highly toxic environments.
  - *Direct Cause 2:* Degradable land storage technologies failing to leverage deep-sea abyssal isolation.

### Objective Tree (Means-End)
- **SUPREME GOAL:** Long-term biosphere protection by transferring environmental risk to isolated abyssal marine zones.
  - *Sub-goal 1:* Total elimination of human exposure in high-danger zones via complete automation.
  - *Sub-goal 2:* Establishing a global standard for smart, corrosion-resistant submarine containers.
- **CENTRAL OBJECTIVE:** Development and validation of the MAR-ROV fleet and hermetic container technology.
  - *Direct Means 1:* Designing robotic arms with advanced haptic feedback and AI for waste identification and handling.
  - *Direct Means 2:* Geological and oceanographic validation of secure deep-sea storage zones.

- **A3.2:** Pilot marine expedition for experimental container deployment at depth (**Milestone 3 - Successful Sea Test**).
- **A4.1:** Consortium management, technical auditing, and European Patent Office (EPO) filings (**Milestone 4 - Final Patent & Report**).

## 6. Ishikawa (Fishbone) Risk Analysis
- **Extraordinary Marine Environment:** Hydrostatic pressure causing micro-cracks; extreme salinity accelerating corrosion.
- **Technology (Machines):** Loss of acoustic/sonar signal at depth; battery degradation in cold waters ($2^\circ\text{C}$).
- **Human Element:** Shortage of deep-sea robotics experts; resistance from traditional environmental groups.
- **Measurements:** IoT sensor failure under pressure, leading to loss of live integrity monitoring.

---

## 7. SWOT Analysis
- **Strengths:** 100% elimination of human risk; abyssal zones are geologically stable and shielded from surface climate events or wars.
- **Weaknesses:** High cost of deep-submergence tech; difficulty repairing ROVs at 3,000 meters depth.
- **Opportunities:** Substantial funding from governments needing nuclear/chemical waste management; establishing an open global standard.
- **Threats:** International maritime conventions (e.g., London Convention) — project must prove non-polluting sealed encapsulation rather than dumping.
